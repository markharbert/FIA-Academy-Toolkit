ClickFunnels one-click login trusts email alone and allows full account takeover
Critical
Authentication and Session Hardening
Anyone who knows an active member's email address can log into that member's Toolkit account without a password, a ClickFunnels session, or any proof they control the email. That gives an attacker direct access to the victim's private account data and lets them act as that user.

The public POST /api/auth/cf-login-ajax route in server/routes.ts:524-605 accepts an arbitrary email address from the request body, lowercases it, and then calls checkCfEnrollmentByEmail(email, ...) to ask ClickFunnels whether that email belongs to an enrolled member. If ClickFunnels says the email is enrolled, the route either loads the existing local user for that email or auto-provisions a new one, then immediately authenticates the caller by assigning req.session.userId = user!.id at server/routes.ts:592. There is no password check, no signed one-time token from ClickFunnels, no proof that the caller owns the submitted email address, and no verification that the request originated from an authenticated ClickFunnels browser session.

The helper in server/clickfunnels.ts:217-287 confirms this trust boundary mistake: checkCfEnrollmentByEmail is only an enrollment lookup. It determines whether an email appears in the configured course's enrollment list, but it does not authenticate the caller or assert that the person making the request controls that ClickFunnels account. Treating a positive enrollment lookup as proof of identity turns membership metadata into a login credential.

This is exploitable from the public internet with a single request. An attacker only needs to know or guess a victim member's email address and send it to /api/auth/cf-login-ajax. The server then creates a valid authenticated Toolkit session for the victim account in the attacker's browser. Because this route is intended to log users in, the impact is full account compromise for any enrolled member: the attacker can read private data exposed through authenticated routes, create or modify user-owned content, generate downstream access such as webhooks, and otherwise operate as the victim until the session is revoked.
